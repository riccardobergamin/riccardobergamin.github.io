HLA Loss and Immune Escape in AML Post-Transplantation

Timing of HLA loss relapses in AML after allo-HCT and the role of ganciclovir

Overview

HLA loss is an important mechanism of immune escape and relapse in acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) following allogeneic haematopoietic cell transplantation (allo-HCT).

In this project, we analysed longitudinal whole-genome sequencing (WGS) data from AML patients to investigate when HLA-loss relapses emerge and to characterise the evolutionary processes underlying these events.

Approach

We combined longitudinal genomic data with clonal evolution analysis, mutational signatures, molecular clocks, mathematical modelling, and Bayesian inference to reconstruct the timing of HLA loss relative to allo-HCT and the emergence of therapy-resistant clones.

We further integrated immunophenotyping, Western blotting, RNA sequencing, and long-read sequencing to investigate the biological relationship between antiviral treatment, DNA damage, and HLA loss in leukaemia cells.

The association between ganciclovir exposure and HLA-loss relapse was subsequently evaluated in three independent clinical cohorts.

Key findings

In 9 of 12 cases, the HLA-loss clone originated early after allo-HCT, indicating that immune-escape variants predominantly emerge during the early post-transplant period.

A distinctive C>A mutational signature was identified in HLA-loss clones and linked to exposure to the antiviral drug ganciclovir.

Mechanistic analyses showed that ganciclovir-induced DNA damage promotes repair through the homologous recombination pathway, favouring the emergence of HLA-loss clones.

The association between ganciclovir exposure and HLA-loss relapse was validated in three independent clinical cohorts.

Impact

This work shows how concomitant therapies can shape cancer evolution and contribute to the emergence of therapy-resistant clones.

The findings provide a mechanistic link between antiviral treatment and immune escape after allo-HCT, with potential implications for antiviral strategies following partially HLA-incompatible transplantation.
