## Overview

Patient-derived organoids (PDOs) provide experimental models of disease that can be readily manipulated for functional characterisation and validation while retaining many of the original features of the human tissues from which they are derived.

However, comprehensive biobanks of characterised and clinically relevant organoid models for use by the wider scientific community remain limited.

In this project, we analysed a live biobank of 43 patient-derived colorectal cancer organoid models generated from primary and metastatic lesions collected from patients receiving standard-of-care treatment or participating in clinical trials.

A subset of models was derived from multiple lesions from the same patient collected longitudinally during treatment.

## Approach

We performed full genomic characterisation of the organoid models using whole-genome sequencing (WGS), complemented by matched single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) for a subset of 20 models.

The genomic and transcriptomic profiles of the organoid models were evaluated against the molecular landscape of colorectal cancer to assess their ability to recapitulate the characteristics of the disease.

## Key findings

The biobank reflects the genomic and transcriptomic landscape of colorectal cancer, providing molecularly characterised models derived from both primary and metastatic lesions.

The longitudinal collection of models from multiple lesions of the same patient further provides a resource for studying tumour evolution across treatment.

## Impact

This work provides a comprehensively characterised live biobank of patient-derived colorectal cancer organoids that can serve as a clinically relevant resource for the wider scientific community.

The combination of whole-genome sequencing and matched single-cell RNA sequencing provides a detailed molecular characterisation of these models for future studies of colorectal cancer biology and evolution.
