## Overview

Clonal evolution and cellular plasticity are fundamental drivers of tumour dynamics, yet they are often studied separately.

PEPI (Population Epigenetics and Phenotypic Inference) is a population epigenetics framework that integrates genetic evolution and reversible phenotypic switching to study how tumour cell populations evolve over time.

We applied PEPI to chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (CLL) with bimodal expression of the integrin alpha 4 subunit (CD49d), a distinct subset in which the CD49d-positive population expands over time and is associated with adverse prognosis.

## Approach

PEPI uses somatic mutations as lineage barcodes to infer clonal fitness and reversible phenotypic switching from single-cell or bulk sequencing data.

We applied the framework to longitudinal whole-genome sequencing data from CD49d-sorted populations collected from eight untreated patients, combining genetic information with phenotypic trajectories to quantify the interplay between clonal selection and cellular plasticity.

We further integrated transcriptomic and chromatin-accessibility data to investigate the molecular mechanisms associated with CD49d expression.

## Key findings

CD49d-positive and CD49d-negative cells arose from shared genetic lineages, indicating that distinct phenotypic states can coexist within the same evolutionary lineages.

CD49d expression was associated with a median fitness advantage of **0.36**, while switching rates varied by orders of magnitude among coexisting clones. These results show that similar phenotypic trajectories can arise from distinct combinations of genetic selection and cellular plasticity.

Transcriptomic and chromatin-accessibility analyses identified a highly localised regulatory programme centred on **ITGA4**, rather than global molecular reprogramming.

## Impact

PEPI provides a quantitative framework for integrating genetic selection and non-genetic plasticity in tumour evolution, enabling the study of how clonal fitness and reversible phenotypic states jointly shape cancer dynamics.
